# Sex-Crime-Related Criminal Cases Involving Prominent U.S. Officeholders, 1976–2026

## Scope and methodology

This report covers cases located in open-web research from 1976 through 2026 involving only the following office categories: presidents or vice presidents (if any), U.S. senators, U.S. representatives, state governors, and mayors of major or nationally or regionally prominent cities. It excludes state legislators, attorneys general, comptrollers, prosecutors, judges, school-board members, city-council members, and small-town or otherwise local cases lacking broader prominence.

“Every” in this report means every case located under that explicit scope in the research materials supplied for this report. Open-web research cannot prove literal completeness: records may be sealed, poorly indexed, unavailable online, described under different terminology, or omitted from accessible databases and news archives. Inclusion therefore reflects the verified results provided, not a claim that no other qualifying case exists.

The report distinguishes arrests, indictments, complaints, allegations, guilty pleas, convictions, dismissals, and appellate outcomes. A person is not called a “pedophile” or “sex offender” unless the cited legal record supports that characterization. Alleged conduct is described as alleged, reported, or charged unless established by a plea, conviction, or other legal finding.

## Included cases by office level and chronology

### U.S. President

#### Donald J. Trump — 45th and 47th U.S. President

- **Office and status:** 45th President (2017–2021) and 47th President (2025–). The civil verdict was returned in May 2023, while he was a former president and an active candidate for the 2024 election. [58]
- **Jurisdiction:** U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York (Manhattan).
- **Nature of proceeding:** Civil suit, not a criminal case. There was no arrest, criminal charge, or criminal conviction. This entry is a clearly labeled exception to the report's criminal-case scope, included because the underlying conduct finding and the trial judge's characterization of it as rape are matters of public record concerning a current U.S. president.
- **Claim:** Writer E. Jean Carroll's civil battery and defamation suit accused Trump of pinning her against a dressing-room wall in a Bergdorf Goodman department store in the mid-1990s and forcibly penetrating her with his fingers. Her allegation was tried to a federal jury. [58][59]
- **Outcome:** On May 9, 2023, the jury found Trump liable for sexual abuse and defamation and awarded Carroll $5 million; it did not find liability under New York law's narrow statutory definition of "rape." On July 19, 2023, Judge Lewis A. Kaplan, denying Trump's request for a new trial, wrote that what the jury found Trump did was in fact rape "as many people commonly understand the word," even though New York Penal Law's definition is narrower. The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit affirmed the verdict on December 30, 2024. [58][59][60]
- **Caution:** This is a civil liability finding on a preponderance-of-the-evidence standard, not a criminal conviction. It should not be described as a criminal charge, a guilty verdict, or a sex-crime conviction. Trump denied the allegation throughout.

### U.S. Representatives

#### Fred (Frederick William) Richmond — U.S. Representative, New York

- **Office and status:** Democrat, New York’s 14th congressional district; served January 2, 1975–August 25, 1982. He was a sitting representative when the D.C. case arose. The House Historian identifies his congressional service. [1][2]
- **Jurisdiction:** Washington, D.C., United States, in D.C. Superior Court; the case was not a Brooklyn/New York prosecution.
- **Arrest/charge date:** April 6, 1978, for the formal arraignment and plea. The available sources establish the arraignment/charge date but do not provide a separate exact arrest date.
- **Alleged offense and charge:** A contemporary *New York Times* report stated that Richmond publicly admitted making paid sexual solicitations to a young man, including a 16-year-old youth, and that he pleaded not guilty in D.C. Superior Court to soliciting a male undercover police officer for prostitution. Later UPI and *New York Times* accounts characterized the matter as soliciting or purchasing sex from a 16-year-old boy and an undercover officer. These are reported admissions, allegations, and charging facts—not a finding that he was convicted of a sex offense. [3][4][5]
- **Outcome:** The 1978 sex-related misdemeanor charge was reported as dismissed under D.C.’s first-offender procedure after Richmond agreed to continue professional or psychological treatment. The sources therefore establish no sex-crime conviction in this case. Richmond later pleaded guilty in August 1982 to unrelated federal charges involving tax evasion, marijuana possession, and an illegal payment to a government employee; he resigned from the House and was sentenced to one year and one day plus a $20,000 fine. Those later convictions should not be conflated with the 1978 sex case. [4][5][6]
- **Caution:** The exact statutory citation and a separate arrest date were not located. Later accounts vary in their description of the 16-year-old’s role; the contemporaneous account distinguishes Richmond’s reported admission from the filed misdemeanor charge involving an undercover adult police officer.

#### Robert E. (Bob) Bauman — U.S. Representative, Maryland

- **Office and status:** Maryland’s 1st congressional district; served August 21, 1973–January 3, 1981. He was a sitting representative when charged and was also president of the American Conservative Union in 1980. [7][8]
- **Jurisdiction:** District of Columbia, United States; Bauman represented Maryland.
- **Arrest/charge date:** October 3, 1980, for the documented formal court appearance and plea. Contemporary later reporting confirms an arrest in October 1980, but the exact arrest date is not established by the reviewed sources.
- **Alleged offense and charge:** Bauman pleaded innocent in D.C. Superior Court to the charge described in contemporaneous UPI reporting as “solicting for lewd and immoral purposes” (the apparent contemporaneous spelling; commonly rendered “soliciting for lewd and immoral purposes”). The charge involved soliciting sex from a 16-year-old boy. UPI reported that police alleged Bauman approached the youth in March and performed oral sodomy; *The Washington Post* likewise reported a solicitation charge involving a teenage boy. The reported conduct was an allegation, not an adjudicated fact. [9][10][11]
- **Outcome:** He entered a court-supervised six-month treatment/counseling program for first offenders and pleaded innocent. The government formally dropped or dismissed the charges on April 3, 1981, after the counseling arrangement. The reviewed sources report no conviction or trial adjudication. [12]
- **Caution:** The exact arrest date and precise statutory citation were not located in an accessible court record. The final disposition was dismissal after counseling, not conviction.

#### Donald E. (Buz) Lukens — U.S. Representative, Ohio

- **Office and status:** U.S. Representative from Ohio; served January 3, 1987–October 24, 1990. He was a sitting representative when indicted and convicted. [13]
- **Jurisdiction:** Franklin County, Ohio, United States.
- **Arrest/charge date:** Formal indictment returned February 23, 1989. The reviewed sources establish an indictment but do not establish that he was physically arrested in this case.
- **Alleged offense and charge:** Lukens was indicted on a misdemeanor charge described as contributing to the delinquency and unruliness of a minor. It arose from an alleged November 6, 1988 sexual encounter with a girl who was 16. Contemporary reporting said prosecutors alleged that he gave her alcohol, paid for a cab to his apartment, and had sex with her knowing she was underage; Lukens denied the allegations. The adjudicated offense was the Ohio child-unruliness/delinquency misdemeanor, not a separately titled statutory-rape or sexual-assault conviction. [14][15]
- **Outcome:** A jury convicted Lukens on May 26, 1989 of contributing to the delinquency and unruliness of a minor. He was sentenced to 30 days in jail and fined $500. The 10th Ohio District Court of Appeals upheld the conviction on June 12, 1990, holding that other evidence supported it. The House biography records that he remained a representative until resigning on October 24, 1990. [16][17]
- **Caution:** Reports vary slightly between “contributing to the unruliness and delinquency of a child” and “contributing to the unruliness of a minor.” The reported sexual conduct and surrounding details were prosecution evidence or allegations; the legally established outcome is the misdemeanor conviction and its affirmance.

#### Mel Reynolds — U.S. Representative, Illinois

- **Office and status:** Democrat, Illinois’s 2nd Congressional District; served January 3, 1993–October 1, 1995, when he resigned. He was a sitting representative when indicted. [18][19]
- **Jurisdiction:** Cook County, Illinois, United States.
- **Arrest/charge date:** Formal indictment returned August 19, 1994. Reynolds surrendered and appeared in court August 22, 1994, and was released on his own recognizance.
- **Alleged offense and charge:** The indictment reportedly contained 20 counts. Contemporary reports described charges involving aggravated criminal sexual abuse, criminal sexual assault, child pornography, solicitation of child pornography, and obstruction-related offenses. Reynolds denied the allegations. The Illinois appellate opinion records the counts that went to the jury: three aggravated-sexual-abuse counts, three criminal-sexual-assault counts, two child-pornography counts, and four obstruction-of-justice counts. [20][21][22][23]
- **Outcome:** A Cook County jury returned guilty verdicts on August 22, 1995, on all 12 counts submitted to it. The trial court imposed four years’ imprisonment for sexual assault, four concurrent years for child pornography, and a consecutive one year for obstruction; the sexual-abuse conviction merged with sexual assault and received no separate sentence. On September 28, 1995, the court sentenced Reynolds to an aggregate five years in prison. The Illinois Appellate Court affirmed in 1997. Reynolds resigned his House seat effective October 1, 1995. [24][25][26]
- **Caution:** The initial indictment-count breakdown differs across contemporaneous summaries because the case began with 20 counts and 12 counts embracing four offenses went to the jury. The appellate opinion controls the adjudicated count description. “Statutory rape” and “soliciting child pornography” were contemporaneous descriptions of indictment allegations, not substitutes for the final verdict labels.

### U.S. Senator

#### Larry Craig — U.S. Senator, Idaho

- **Office and status:** U.S. Senator from Idaho, serving January 1991–January 2009; sitting senator at the time of the incident and plea.
- **Jurisdiction:** Minneapolis–St. Paul International Airport and Hennepin County, Minnesota.
- **Arrest/charge date:** June 11, 2007, when Craig was arrested in an airport restroom sting. Police prepared a formal complaint for disturbing-the-peace/disorderly conduct and interference with privacy. He entered a guilty plea on August 8, 2007. [27][28]
- **Alleged offense and charge:** A plainclothes officer investigating complaints of sexual or lewd activity alleged that Craig looked into the stall, tapped and moved his foot toward the officer’s foot, touched the officer’s foot, and reached under the divider. Craig denied soliciting sex and said the conduct was misunderstood. The documented charges were disturbing-the-peace/disorderly conduct and interference with privacy; no formal sex-offense charge is shown in the reviewed court records. [27][29][30]
- **Outcome:** Craig pleaded guilty by written plea to misdemeanor disorderly conduct on August 8, 2007; the interference-with-privacy charge was dismissed. He received a $1,000 fine, $575 in court fees, one year of unsupervised probation, and a 10-day jail sentence that was stayed. The district court denied his attempt to withdraw the plea, and the Minnesota Court of Appeals affirmed on December 9, 2008. [28][31]
- **Scope qualification:** This entry is included under the stated arrest-or-formal-charge test because Craig was a sitting U.S. senator arrested in a police sting concerning alleged sexual or lewd conduct, with national political and ethics consequences. If the scope were limited only to formal sex-crime charges or sex-crime convictions, he would be excluded: the documented charges and conviction were disorderly conduct and interference with privacy, not a sex offense. He should not be described as convicted of a sex crime based on this record.

### State Governors

#### Eric Greitens — Governor of Missouri

- **Office and status:** Governor of Missouri; sitting governor at the time of arrest and indictment, elected in 2016.
- **Jurisdiction:** St. Louis, Missouri, United States.
- **Arrest/charge date:** February 22, 2018. A St. Louis grand jury indicted Greitens that day, and he was arrested/booked and released on his own recognizance. [32][33]
- **Alleged offense and charge:** Felony invasion of privacy. The indictment alleged that on March 21, 2015, during an extramarital affair, Greitens knowingly photographed a woman identified by initials while she was in a state of full or partial nudity, without her knowledge or consent, and transmitted the image in a manner that allowed access via a computer. Reporting described an alleged secretly taken compromising photograph. The reported threat to publish the image or blackmail the woman was part of surrounding allegations; it was not itself part of the indictment according to NPR. [32][33][34]
- **Outcome:** The St. Louis prosecutor dismissed or withdrew the felony invasion-of-privacy charge on May 14, 2018, during jury selection and before trial, citing a conflict created by the judge’s ruling that the defense could call Circuit Attorney Kim Gardner as a witness. Prosecutors initially said they would refile through a special prosecutor. On June 8, 2018, the special prosecutor decided not to refile because of insufficient corroborating evidence. Greitens was never convicted or acquitted on this charge; the case ended without a trial adjudication. He resigned as governor effective June 1, 2018, separately from the criminal disposition. [35][36][37]
- **Caution:** The alleged photograph, nonconsensual circumstances, restraint, and threat to disseminate it were allegations or witness accounts, not adjudicated facts. The Missouri House investigative report stated that it possessed no physical or electronic evidence of the photograph or its transmission. Greitens acknowledged the affair but denied criminal wrongdoing and disputed the blackmail allegation.

#### Andrew Cuomo — Governor of New York

- **Office and status:** Governor of New York, serving 2011–August 2021; a nationally prominent former three-term governor when the criminal complaint was filed.
- **Jurisdiction:** Albany City Court and Albany County, New York.
- **Arrest/charge date:** October 28, 2021. The Albany County Sheriff’s Office filed a misdemeanor criminal complaint, and Albany City Court issued a criminal summons ordering Cuomo to appear on November 17, 2021. The sources confirm the summons and charge but do not establish a custodial arrest. [38][39]
- **Alleged offense and charge:** Forcible touching, a New York Class A misdemeanor and sex offense under Penal Law § 130.52. The complaint alleged that on December 7, 2020, at the Executive Mansion, Cuomo put his hand under the complainant’s blouse and onto an intimate body part, fondled or grabbed her breast, and did so to degrade or abuse her or gratify sexual desire. This is the complaint’s allegation, not an adjudicated fact. [40][41]
- **Outcome:** On January 4, 2022, Albany County District Attorney David Soares said he was seeking dismissal because, although the complainant was considered cooperative and credible, prosecutors could not meet their burden of proof at trial. On January 7, 2022, Judge Holly Trexler dismissed the only criminal charge. Cuomo denied the allegation. There was no conviction, plea, or adjudicated finding that he committed the alleged touching. [40][42]
- **Caution:** The formal filing is verified, but the available sources verify a summons rather than a custodial arrest. The criminal dismissal ended the case without a merits determination of guilt or innocence. Separate administrative or attorney-general findings should not be presented as a criminal conviction.

### Mayors of major or regionally prominent cities

#### Dennis (Denny) Doyle — Mayor of Beaverton, Oregon

- **Office and status:** Mayor of Beaverton, a substantial Portland metropolitan suburb; served 2009–2020. AP reports that he was elected in 2008 and lost reelection in 2020. [43]
- **Jurisdiction:** Beaverton, Oregon, and the U.S. District Court for the District of Oregon.
- **Arrest/charge date:** March 3, 2022, when a federal criminal information was filed. DOJ announced the charge March 4 and stated that Doyle would make his first federal-court appearance that day. The reviewed sources do not establish a separate initial arrest date. [44]
- **Alleged offense and charge:** One federal count of possession of child pornography. DOJ stated that, between November 2014 and December 2015, Doyle allegedly knowingly possessed digital material containing child pornography, including images depicting minors under 12. The initial information was an accusation and carried a presumption of innocence. [44]
- **Outcome:** Doyle pleaded guilty in federal court on October 11, 2022 to the single possession count. On January 24, 2023, he was sentenced to six months in federal prison, five years of supervised release, and $22,000 restitution; AP reported that he was required to register as a sex offender. The guilty plea and sentencing establish an adjudicated conviction. [45][46]
- **Caution:** The DOJ releases charge only possession; they do not establish separate charges for production, distribution, or hands-on sexual abuse. The report uses “child pornography” here because that is the offense terminology in the cited legal releases.

#### Patrick Wojahn — Mayor of College Park, Maryland

- **Office and status:** Former mayor of College Park, a Maryland city of roughly 35,000 people in the Washington, D.C., metropolitan area; served from 2015 until resigning immediately before his March 2, 2023 arrest.
- **Jurisdiction:** College Park, Prince George’s County, Maryland, United States.
- **Arrest/charge date:** March 2, 2023. Wojahn was arrested and initially charged with 56 counts: 40 counts of possession of child-exploitative material and 16 counts of distribution of child-exploitative material. [47][48]
- **Alleged offense and charge:** The initial police charges concerned child sexual-abuse material. The later criminal case resulted in a guilty plea to 100 counts of knowing possession of child pornography with intent to distribute under Maryland Criminal Law § 11-207 and 40 counts of knowing possession of child pornography under § 11-208, for 140 counts total. [49][50]
- **Outcome:** Wojahn pleaded guilty on August 2, 2023 to 140 child-pornography/child-sexual-abuse-material counts. On November 20, 2023, the court sentenced him to 150 years, with all but 30 years stayed, resulting in a 30-year prison term. The official District of Columbia Board on Professional Responsibility report records the plea, statutory sections, and sentence; *The Washington Post* independently reported the 30-year prison term. [51][52]
- **Caution:** The initial police allegations and 56-count arrest description are distinct from the later 140-count adjudicated plea. News reports use varying shorthand, while the official disciplinary report supplies the statutory wording and final count structure.

### Former U.S. Representative and mayoral candidate with post-office federal conviction

#### Anthony Weiner — Former U.S. Representative, New York

- **Office and status:** Democrat, New York’s 9th Congressional District; served January 3, 1999–June 21, 2011, resigning during the 112th Congress. He later ran for mayor of New York City, but the criminal case concerned conduct after his congressional service. [53][54]
- **Jurisdiction:** U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York, Manhattan/New York.
- **Arrest/charge date:** May 19, 2017. DOJ reported that Weiner surrendered to the FBI in New York, was charged, and pleaded guilty in federal court the same day. The reviewed sources do not establish a separate earlier physical-arrest date. [55]
- **Alleged offense and charge:** One federal count described by DOJ as transferring obscene material to a minor. DOJ stated that, between approximately January and March 2016, Weiner used online messaging and video-chat applications to communicate with a girl he knew was 15 and transferred obscene material, including sexually explicit images and directions to engage in sexual conduct. [55][56]
- **Outcome:** Weiner pleaded guilty and was convicted on May 19, 2017. On September 25, 2017, he was sentenced in Manhattan federal court to 21 months in federal prison and three years of supervised release. Reuters reported that he served about 14 months and was released in February 2019. [56][57]
- **Caution:** The conviction is established by the guilty plea and sentencing. Descriptions of the 2016 exchanges should be attributed to DOJ’s filed-information and court-proceeding account rather than described as independently tried jury findings.

## Exclusions and uncertainties

The explicit office filter excludes state legislators, attorneys general, comptrollers, prosecutors, judges, school-board members, city-council members, and small-town or purely local cases lacking broader prominence. Cases based only on civil lawsuits, workplace or ethics findings, rumors, media reports without a formal criminal case, or social-media claims were not included as criminal cases. Reddit, if encountered as a lead, is not evidence and is not relied upon here.

Larry Craig is included only because the requested scope expressly permits arrest or formal-charge cases involving sitting senators and because the arrest concerned alleged sexual or lewd conduct with extensive national consequences; his documented conviction was not for a sex offense. Greitens is included as a formal felony invasion-of-privacy case with alleged nude-image conduct, while noting that it was not a rape or sexual-assault charge and ended without adjudication. Cuomo is included because the formal forcible-touching complaint was a statutory sex-offense charge, but the summons should not be relabeled as a custodial arrest. Doyle’s initial charge was a federal criminal information rather than a verified initial arrest, but the later guilty plea and sentence are established. Weiner’s conduct and conviction occurred after his congressional service; he is included because the supplied scope includes nationally prominent officeholders and the cited DOJ release identifies him as a former congressman and mayoral candidate.

Donald J. Trump is the sole civil-case entry: his case involved no arrest or criminal charge and is included as a labeled exception because a federal jury's liability finding and the trial judge's published characterization of the conduct as rape, as commonly understood, are matters of public record concerning a sitting U.S. president. It must not be presented as a criminal case.

The supplied results contain no additional candidate that remained both within the stated office filter and unverified after review. Accordingly, the report records **0 unresolved included candidates** in the supplied result set; this is not a claim that open-web research has located every possible case.
